In one of  the recent Latent Space podcast with Cline, Alessio discussed he thinks MCP is going to cannibalize SaaS. He doesn't even bother paying for Sentry's debugging AI agent, but just pulls the raw logs for free from Sentry's MCP and lets his IDE's AI agent debug for him. A friend of mine also rather copy and paste his Notion content to Grok than paying for Notion AI. They already paid for their coding agent, Grok subscription. Why subscribe to another again?

I see most of "vertical AI" for most SaaS products as a losing battle. To me, the real AI-native company is not the company that builds AI agents into their services, but makes their products easily usable by AI, not just humans. And I think that is the real moat. Not calling APIs or building some scaffolding to solve a vertical/niche issue.

Besides the unmatched efforts inference providers put in to perfect their AI agent, one factor I don't see people discuss enough is cost. People estimate most of API margins are around 80%. That is why you see many Claude Code users saves thousands by subscribing to Claude Max compared to raw API calls. This abundance allows inference providers to build agents and engineer context in a financially reasonable way that even Cursor can hardly compete.

I think an easy way to tell if a product is going to be consumed by inference providers is if your product is AI complete. While many encourage you to build AI complete companies to easily benefit whenever the model improves, the reality is yes, you improve, but also get replaced. Instead, I think products that get "enabled" but not necessarily "completed" by AI are safer.
People often say you should build Uber instead of flashlight in the AI era. To me, Uber is iPhone-enabled, but not iPhone-complete. It means today even if you have the best/complete iPhone, you still need Uber's fleet. This isn't the same for many camera apps.

This is why I am bullish on most of the pre-AI SaaS companies over post-AI era companies. This doesn't mean they don't need to adapt. Their efforts should focus not on how to build an AI agent but optimize their products/business models to not just abstract away complexity from humans but also AI.
Companies that want to hoard the users/records to their own platform with their own AI will easily be replaced by companies that are willing to join the ecosystem. Similar to how you get ranked highly on Google if you allow them to scrape your content.

I don't think Sentry would be replaced by AI-native companies (Alessio still need "Sentry MCP"), but many AI debugging companies would be replaced by OpenAI. VSCode will still dominate but I doubt Cursor can continue to grow strong.

Building tools, not AI agents, is probably what most products should focus on
