ALL in MCP

Recently, we decided to stop most of our frontend work for our workflow builder on our website, and instead fully focus on MCP.

Yeah, it’s a bit reckless. And honestly, it might turn out to be a huge mistake. 

But like Brian Balfour said in his interview with Lenny, startups don’t get the luxury to try many things at once. You need to find your conviction and go all in. We felt the pain of building both MCP and our traditional UI/UX. Even with the same API backend, the design principles are quite different. It kind of naturally leads us to decide on one and commit.

I do want to share some of the conviction and observations we have that leads to this decision. 

1. Magical moment: 
Even with all the criticisms of MCP, I still remember how I felt the first time I plugged in an MCP and suddenly the LLM just worked for me. I could choose what I wanted to use, without relying on provider’s “priority” for integrations. The LLM + MCP create a smooth experience to bring my thought to action that I have never experienced before, and it felt like living in the future. That "magical moment" is the main reason we want to go all in and to build.

2. Behavioral change: 
The real test is: do I often find myself wishing I had MCP in my workflow? A successful product changes behavior. Now we create tickets in Claude, get user info from Claude, and I get annoyed when I run out of context. I also frustrated that we use Fly’s Postgres instead of Supabase or Neon, who has native MCP supports to let me quickly build my queries. This kind of frustration shows me that MCP is shaping my expectations, and that drives behaviors change.

3. ChatGPT and Claude are the iPhone, not AWS: 
It's no secret they aren’t trying to be just infrastructure. They are building ecosystems, like Apple did. Their North Star now is active users, and they design for their largest user base. They add features constantly to keep people on the platform(Honestly, once they figure out how to linearly turn screen time into revenue, I don’t think they’ll have much financial incentive to support API products, just like google search api). If the LLM client is the iOS, then MCP is the app. And if this platform grows fast, building native apps on it gives a big advantage over those who just “keeps a seat.”

My thesis is that whenever there’s a new way to access information, a new platform will appear to natively serve that information consuming process, and that platform will create an ecosystem. 
1. GUI -> The rise of simple way to control data -> PC -> Desktop APPs.
2. Internet -> The rise of access world wide data -> Browser -> Web Apps
3. Mobile -> The rise of access internet data on the go -> iPhone -> Mobile Apps
4. LLM -> The rise of access personalized synthesized information -> ChatGPT/Claude -> (MCP)? 

This is my thesis. But who knows
