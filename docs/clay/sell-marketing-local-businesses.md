# How I'd Sell Marketing Services to Local Businesses Using Clay

*Google Maps reviews + social media gaps = the signal chain that closes local deals.*

---

If you run a marketing agency or freelance as a social media manager, you know the grind: cold call local businesses, pitch them on "growing their online presence," and hear some version of "we're fine" from someone who hasn't posted on Instagram in six months.

The problem isn't your offer. It's that you're pitching blind. You don't know *which* businesses actually need help, or *what specific thing* they're failing at. So every pitch sounds generic and every prospect feels cold.

What if you could walk into the conversation already knowing their Google rating is 3.2 stars, they haven't posted on social media in 4 months, and they're running zero ads -- while their competitor down the street has a 4.8 rating and posts daily?

That's not a cold call anymore. That's a consultation.

## The Setup: Google Maps as Your Lead Source

Forget buying lead lists. Clay connects directly to Google Maps. Type in "dentists in Austin, Texas" or "coffee shops in Brooklyn" and you get a ready-made list of local businesses with names, addresses, phone numbers, and review data.

This is your base list. Real businesses, real locations, real contact information. No scraping, no manual lookup.

## The Signal Chain

**Layer 1 -- Base list from Google Maps**

Import 200 dental practices (or restaurants, or salons, or whatever niche you serve) from your target city. Clay pulls the business name, address, phone, website, Google rating, and review count automatically.

**Layer 2 -- Digital presence audit (fit signal)**

Add a Claygent column:

> "Visit this company's website. Check if they have active Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, and Facebook accounts linked. For each platform found, note the approximate posting frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, inactive). Also check if they appear to be running Google Ads or Meta Ads."

In one pass, you now know which businesses are actually investing in marketing and which are completely dark. The ones posting once a month with no ads? Those are your prospects.

**Layer 3 -- Pain signal from reviews**

Add another AI column to analyze their Google reviews:

> "Look at the most recent 10 Google reviews for this business. Identify any negative themes: long wait times, poor customer service, outdated facilities, pricing complaints. Summarize the top 2-3 complaint patterns."

Why does this matter for a marketing agency? Because a business with bad reviews and no social media presence is *bleeding customers they don't even know about*. You can show them exactly where they're losing -- and how content, reputation management, or ads can fix it.

**Layer 4 -- Competitive context**

The killer move: pull the same data for their top competitor.

> "Find the highest-rated competitor within 5 miles of this business in the same category. Return their Google rating, review count, and social media posting frequency."

Now your outreach isn't "you should do more marketing." It's "your competitor has a 4.8 rating with 340 reviews and posts on Instagram daily. You have a 3.4 with 45 reviews and haven't posted in 3 months. Here's the gap, and here's what it's costing you."

## Why This Works for Any Local Niche

The template is the same whether you sell to:
- **Restaurants**: review complaints about wait times + no online ordering presence
- **Salons**: low Google rating + no Instagram portfolio + competitor comparison
- **Gyms**: negative reviews about equipment + no YouTube content + competitor class offerings
- **Auto shops**: payment complaints in reviews + no website + competitor pricing transparency

Swap the niche, keep the signal chain. Import from Google Maps, audit their digital presence, mine their reviews for pain, and pull their competitor's data for contrast. The framework scales to any local service business.
